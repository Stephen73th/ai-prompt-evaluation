# AI Response Evaluation Scoring Rubric

## Project
Prompt Matters: A Comparative Evaluation of AI Tools and the Impact of Prompt Design on Output Quality

## 1. Purpose

This scoring rubric provides a consistent framework for evaluating the responses generated by ChatGPT and Google Gemini across the five experimental tasks and two prompt conditions.

The rubric is designed to minimise arbitrary judgement and ensure that both platforms are evaluated using the same standards.

## 2. Evaluation Criteria

Each response will be assessed against five criteria.

### Criterion 1 — Accuracy

Measures the factual correctness of the response, including whether information, explanations, calculations, or reasoning are reliable.

For creative writing, accuracy will focus on narrative consistency and coherence rather than factual correctness.

### Criterion 2 — Relevance

Measures how directly the response addresses the task and whether its content remains focused on the requested subject.

### Criterion 3 — Completeness

Measures whether the response includes the essential information and satisfies the major requirements of the prompt.

### Criterion 4 — Clarity

Measures how understandable, well-organised, and effectively communicated the response is.

### Criterion 5 — Instruction Following

Measures how well the AI adheres to explicit requirements, including word limits, formatting, tone, structure, restrictions, and requested content.

## 3. Scoring Scale

Every criterion will be scored from 1 to 5.

| Score | Rating | General Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Very Poor | Major failures; requirement largely unmet |
| 2 | Poor | Significant weaknesses; requirement only partially met |
| 3 | Acceptable | Adequate performance with noticeable shortcomings |
| 4 | Good | Strong performance with minor shortcomings |
| 5 | Excellent | Outstanding performance; requirement fully and effectively met |

## 4. Detailed Scoring Guidance

### Accuracy

| Score | Guidance |
|---|---|
| 1 | Contains major factual or logical errors |
| 2 | Contains several significant errors |
| 3 | Mostly correct but contains some inaccuracies or weaknesses |
| 4 | Accurate with only minor issues |
| 5 | Fully accurate and reliable for the task |

### Relevance

| Score | Guidance |
|---|---|
| 1 | Mostly unrelated to the task |
| 2 | Frequently deviates from the task |
| 3 | Generally relevant but contains unnecessary content |
| 4 | Relevant with minimal deviation |
| 5 | Directly and consistently addresses the task |

### Completeness

| Score | Guidance |
|---|---|
| 1 | Omits most essential requirements |
| 2 | Covers only a limited portion of the requirements |
| 3 | Covers the main requirements but misses some important details |
| 4 | Covers nearly all requirements |
| 5 | Fully satisfies all essential task requirements |

### Clarity

| Score | Guidance |
|---|---|
| 1 | Difficult to understand or poorly organised |
| 2 | Frequently unclear or confusing |
| 3 | Understandable but unevenly structured |
| 4 | Clear and well-organised |
| 5 | Exceptionally clear, coherent, and easy to follow |

### Instruction Following

| Score | Guidance |
|---|---|
| 1 | Ignores most explicit instructions |
| 2 | Follows few instructions |
| 3 | Follows most instructions but misses or violates some |
| 4 | Follows nearly all instructions with minor deviations |
| 5 | Follows all explicit instructions accurately |

## 5. Response Score Calculation

Each response receives five individual scores.

**Maximum score: 25**

Total Response Score = Accuracy + Relevance + Completeness + Clarity + Instruction Following

### Score Interpretation

| Total | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| 21–25 | Excellent |
| 16–20 | Good |
| 11–15 | Acceptable |
| 6–10 | Poor |
| 5 | Very Poor |

These bands are descriptive summaries for this project and do not replace the individual criterion scores.

## 6. Prompt Improvement Comparison

For each task, the basic-prompt and improved-prompt scores will be compared within the same AI platform.

Prompt Score Difference = Improved Prompt Score − Basic Prompt Score

A positive difference indicates a higher score under the improved-prompt condition. A negative difference indicates a lower score. A difference of zero indicates no change in total score.

The comparison will be interpreted alongside the actual responses and criterion-level scores.

## 7. AI Platform Comparison

ChatGPT and Gemini will be compared using the same task and prompt condition.

Comparisons will examine:

- Total scores.
- Individual criterion scores.
- Quality and usefulness of responses.
- Differences in response structure and style.
- Errors and omissions.
- Compliance with prompt requirements.

A platform will not be declared universally superior based on a single task or score.

## 8. Task-Specific Evaluation Notes

### T1 — Knowledge & Explanation

Focus on factual accuracy, beginner accessibility, quality of analogies, usefulness of examples, and adherence to requested length and structure.

### T2 — Summarisation

Focus on preservation of meaning, inclusion of central ideas, balance, conciseness, and avoidance of information absent from the source.

### T3 — Creative Writing

Focus on narrative coherence, originality, engagement, character development, effective plot construction, and compliance with creative constraints.

For this task, Accuracy will be interpreted as internal narrative consistency and plausibility.

### T4 — Reasoning & Problem Solving

Focus on correctness of the final answer, accuracy of intermediate calculations, logical method, explanation quality, and adherence to requested presentation.

Mathematical results will be independently verified.

### T5 — Complex Instruction Following

Focus on requirement coverage, schedule feasibility, correct budget calculations, structural compliance, and successful fulfilment of explicit instructions.

The improved prompt contains 20 explicit requirements, which will also be evaluated separately using the constraint compliance measure.

## 9. T5 Constraint Compliance Measure

The improved prompt for T5 contains 20 explicitly numbered requirements.

Each requirement will be recorded as:

- Satisfied — 1
- Not satisfied — 0

Constraint Compliance Rate:

(Requirements Satisfied ÷ Total Requirements) × 100

For example, if an AI satisfies 17 of 20 requirements:

(17 ÷ 20) × 100 = 85%

The compliance rate will be reported separately from the five-criterion response score.

## 10. Scoring Record Template

Each response should have a completed scoring record.

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| Experiment ID | T1–T5 |
| AI Platform | ChatGPT or Gemini |
| Prompt Type | Basic or Improved |
| Test Date | Date of execution |
| Accuracy | Score 1–5 |
| Relevance | Score 1–5 |
| Completeness | Score 1–5 |
| Clarity | Score 1–5 |
| Instruction Following | Score 1–5 |
| Total | Score out of 25 |
| Observations | Evidence supporting the scores |
| Errors / Omissions | Identified shortcomings |

## 11. Evaluator Consistency

To reduce individual bias, squad members should use the same rubric and refer to specific evidence in the response when assigning scores.

Where practical, at least two team members should independently evaluate a response. Differences in judgement should be discussed, and agreed scores or average scores should be recorded consistently.

Evaluators should avoid favouring a platform based on familiarity, brand reputation, or personal preference.

## 12. Reporting Results

Results will be presented using comparative tables and appropriate visualisations.

The analysis may include:

- Average scores for each platform.
- Basic versus improved prompt score differences.
- Performance by task category.
- Criterion-level comparisons.
- T5 constraint compliance percentages.
- Qualitative examples of notable strengths and failures.

The final report or presentation will include interpretations supported by recorded experimental evidence.

## 13. Interpretation and Limitations

Scores represent performance observed under the conditions of this experiment. They do not establish permanent rankings of AI platforms or prove that one prompting strategy will always produce better results.

Differences in platform updates, response variability, sample size, and evaluator judgement will be acknowledged when interpreting the findings.

## 14. Final Evaluation Principle

The evaluation will prioritise evidence over impressions. A response that sounds confident, polished, or sophisticated will not automatically receive a high score unless it demonstrates quality against the agreed criteria.
