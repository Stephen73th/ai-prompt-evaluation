# Experiment Design and Methodology

## 1. Introduction

This document describes the methodology used in the Prompt Matters project to compare generative AI tools and investigate how prompt design influences AI-generated outputs.

The experiment is designed to examine the responses produced by ChatGPT and Google Gemini when given identical tasks using two different prompt strategies: basic prompting and improved prompting.

## 2. Research Approach

The project adopts a practical comparative experimental approach. Both AI platforms will be exposed to the same tasks and prompt versions. Their responses will be collected and evaluated against predefined criteria.

The experiment focuses on observable output quality rather than internal model processes.

## 3. AI Platforms

The platforms selected for the experiment are:

- ChatGPT
- Google Gemini

These tools were selected because they are widely accessible generative AI platforms and can be used to perform a range of tasks, including explanation, summarisation, creative writing, reasoning, and instruction following.

## 4. Experimental Variables

### Independent Variables

The experiment examines two main independent variables:

**AI platform:** ChatGPT and Google Gemini.

**Prompt strategy:** Basic prompts and improved prompts.

### Dependent Variable

The dependent variable is the quality of the AI-generated output, assessed through accuracy, relevance, completeness, clarity, and instruction following.

## 5. Experimental Tasks

Five task categories will be evaluated.

| ID | Task | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | Knowledge & Explanation | Evaluate the quality of explanations |
| T2 | Summarisation | Evaluate information extraction and meaning preservation |
| T3 | Creative Writing | Evaluate creativity and compliance with creative requirements |
| T4 | Reasoning & Problem Solving | Evaluate logical and mathematical correctness |
| T5 | Complex Instruction Following | Evaluate the ability to satisfy multiple constraints |

Each task will contain one basic prompt and one improved prompt.

## 6. Prompt Conditions

### Basic Prompt

The basic prompt provides minimal instructions and limited context. It allows the AI platform considerable freedom to determine the structure, style, depth, and approach of the response.

### Improved Prompt

The improved prompt provides additional direction. Depending on the task, it may specify:

- Role or context.
- Intended audience.
- Clear objective.
- Detailed requirements.
- Output format.
- Length restrictions.
- Quality expectations.
- Constraints and restrictions.

The improved prompts are designed to reduce ambiguity and make the intended output more explicit without changing the underlying task.

## 7. Experimental Procedure

The following procedure will be applied consistently:

1. Select the task to be tested.
2. Copy the basic prompt exactly as documented.
3. Submit the basic prompt to ChatGPT.
4. Save the complete response.
5. Submit the identical basic prompt to Google Gemini.
6. Save the complete response.
7. Repeat the procedure using the improved prompt.
8. Save both improved-prompt responses.
9. Record the date and platform used.
10. Proceed to the next task.

This procedure will be repeated for all five tasks.

## 8. Experiment Size

The experiment consists of:

- 2 AI platforms.
- 5 task categories.
- 2 prompt conditions per task.
- 20 total responses.

Each platform will generate 10 outputs: five basic-prompt responses and five improved-prompt responses.

## 9. Controlled Conditions

To promote a fair comparison:

- Identical prompts will be used across platforms.
- Identical source materials will be provided where required.
- The same task requirements will be maintained.
- Responses will be collected without selectively editing AI outputs.
- The same evaluation criteria will be applied to both platforms.
- Responses will be saved as generated, including errors or omissions.
- The date of testing will be documented.
- No additional context will be provided to one platform unless it is part of the documented prompt.

Where platform settings or model versions differ or cannot be standardised, those differences will be documented as experimental limitations.

## 10. Response Evaluation

Each response will be evaluated using five criteria on a scale from 1 to 5.

| Criterion | Evaluation Focus |
|---|---|
| Accuracy | Correctness and reliability of the response |
| Relevance | How directly the response addresses the task |
| Completeness | Whether required information is covered |
| Clarity | How understandable and organised the response is |
| Instruction Following | How well the response adheres to the prompt |

A score of 1 represents very poor performance, while a score of 5 represents excellent performance.

The maximum score per response is 25.

## 11. Special Evaluation Measures

### Reasoning and Problem Solving

For T4, mathematical answers will be verified independently using the correct calculations. Detailed explanations will not automatically be treated as evidence of correctness.

### Creative Writing

For T3, accuracy will be assessed primarily through narrative coherence, consistency, and adherence to the requested story elements rather than factual correctness.

### Complex Instruction Following

For T5, the 20 explicit requirements in the improved prompt will be checked individually. The constraint compliance rate will be calculated as:

Constraint Compliance Rate = (Requirements Satisfied ÷ Total Requirements) × 100

This measure will complement the general five-criterion scoring rubric.

## 12. Data Recording

The following information will be recorded for each response:

- Experiment ID.
- Task category.
- Prompt type.
- AI platform.
- Date of testing.
- Complete prompt.
- Complete AI response.
- Individual evaluation scores.
- Total score.
- Evaluator observations.
- Errors, omissions, or unexpected behaviours.

## 13. Analysis Plan

The collected results will be analysed by:

1. Comparing basic and improved prompt scores for each platform.
2. Comparing the two platforms using identical prompts.
3. Examining performance across task categories.
4. Identifying recurring errors and instruction-following failures.
5. Identifying tasks where prompt improvements had little or no effect.
6. Summarising qualitative observations alongside numerical scores.
7. Developing evidence-based findings and recommendations.

Numerical results will be interpreted alongside the actual responses to avoid drawing conclusions from scores alone.

## 14. Limitations

The experiment has several limitations:

- Only two AI platforms are being evaluated.
- The experiment includes five task categories and a limited number of prompts.
- AI outputs may vary between runs.
- Platform versions and capabilities may change.
- Some evaluation criteria, especially creativity and usefulness, involve subjective judgement.
- Scores may be influenced by the evaluator's experience and interpretation.
- Findings from this experiment may not apply to every AI tool or task.

The project therefore aims to provide practical observations rather than universal conclusions about generative AI.

## 15. Ethical and Responsible Evaluation

AI responses will be critically reviewed. Generated information will not automatically be considered accurate simply because it is presented confidently.

The experiment will document both successful and unsuccessful outputs fairly. Conclusions will be based on recorded evidence, and limitations will be acknowledged in the final presentation.

## 16. Expected Outcome

The experiment is expected to provide a practical understanding of how different generative AI platforms respond to identical tasks, how prompt structure may influence output quality, and why users should evaluate AI-generated information critically.

The results will form the foundation of the project's final presentation or video.
