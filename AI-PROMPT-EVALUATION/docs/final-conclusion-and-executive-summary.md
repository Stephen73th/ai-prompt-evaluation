# FINAL CONCLUSION AND EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

## PART A: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

### Project Title
**Prompt Matters: A Comparative Evaluation of AI Tools and the Impact of Prompt Design on Output Quality**

### 1. Background

Artificial intelligence tools such as ChatGPT and Google Gemini are increasingly used for learning, writing, problem-solving and everyday tasks. However, the quality of their responses can depend on how users formulate their instructions, commonly known as prompts.

This project investigated how prompt design influences AI-generated output by comparing basic and improved prompts across two AI platforms.

### 2. Aim and Objectives

The main aim was to evaluate the impact of prompt design on the quality of responses generated by ChatGPT and Google Gemini.

The specific objectives were to:

- Compare the responses generated by basic and improved prompts.
- Evaluate the performance of ChatGPT and Google Gemini across different tasks.
- Identify how prompt clarity and specificity influence response quality.
- Examine the strengths and limitations of both platforms.
- Develop practical recommendations for effective AI prompting.

### 3. Methodology

The project adopted a comparative experimental approach involving two AI platforms: ChatGPT and Google Gemini.

Five task categories were selected:

1. Knowledge and explanation.
2. Summarisation.
3. Creative writing.
4. Reasoning and problem-solving.
5. Complex instruction following.

Each task was tested using a basic prompt and an improved prompt on both platforms, producing 20 AI responses.

Responses were assessed using five criteria, each scored from 1 to 5, giving a maximum score of 25 per response.

### 4. Key Results

The experiment produced the following results:

| Evaluation Category | Score | Percentage |
|---|---:|---:|
| ChatGPT | 248/250 | 99.2% |
| Gemini | 245/250 | 98.0% |
| Basic prompts | 245/250 | 98.0% |
| Improved prompts | 248/250 | 99.2% |
| **Overall** | **493/500** | **98.6%** |

Improved prompts achieved a three-point advantage over basic prompts. ChatGPT also achieved a slightly higher overall score than Gemini.

However, both platforms performed strongly, and improved prompts did not produce higher scores in every task.

### 5. Main Findings

The project identified five major findings:

- Improved prompts can increase response quality by providing clearer instructions and expectations.
- Both AI platforms demonstrated strong performance across the selected tasks.
- The impact of prompt improvement varied according to task complexity.
- Basic prompts were sufficient for some straightforward tasks.
- AI-generated responses require human review to identify errors and unmet requirements.

### 6. Recommendations

Users should formulate clear and specific prompts, include measurable output requirements where necessary, and match prompt complexity to the task. They should also review AI-generated responses before using them for important purposes.

### 7. Conclusion

The project demonstrates that prompt design can influence the quality and usefulness of AI-generated responses. However, effective prompting does not eliminate the need for human judgement. The best results come from combining clear instructions, appropriate AI tools and careful evaluation of generated content.

## PART B: FINAL PROJECT CONCLUSION

### 1. Conclusion

This project examined the relationship between prompt design and AI-generated output quality through a comparative evaluation of ChatGPT and Google Gemini. By testing five task categories under basic and improved prompting conditions, the project provided practical evidence of how different prompt structures influence AI responses.

The results showed that improved prompts achieved 248 out of 250, compared with 245 out of 250 for basic prompts. This represents a small but positive difference in favour of improved prompting. ChatGPT achieved 248 out of 250, while Gemini achieved 245 out of 250.

The findings suggest that detailed instructions can help AI systems produce responses that are more structured, relevant and aligned with specific user requirements. Nevertheless, the results also demonstrate that improved prompts do not guarantee better performance in every situation. In some tasks, basic prompts produced equally strong results because the original instructions were already sufficiently clear.

An important observation from the experiments was that both platforms occasionally failed to satisfy specific requirements, such as prescribed word counts. This reinforces the importance of human oversight when using generative AI.

Overall, the project concludes that prompt design is a valuable skill for improving interactions with AI tools. However, the effectiveness of prompting depends on the nature of the task, the quality of the instructions and the ability of the AI platform to interpret and follow those instructions.

The project also highlights the importance of evaluating AI tools through practical tasks rather than relying solely on general performance claims. Although ChatGPT achieved a marginally higher score in this experiment, the difference was small and should not be interpreted as proof of universal superiority.

The central conclusion is that effective AI use requires three complementary elements: clear prompt design, appropriate selection of AI tools and human evaluation of generated outputs.
